I want to substantially rework `trainScene` and `trainLandscape` into one cohesive, immersive experience that feels unmistakably like travelling on a real **Gdańsk SKM train**.

This should not be treated as a simple visual upgrade. I want you to rethink the presentation, density, atmosphere, environmental composition, spatial depth, and overall feeling of these scenes while preserving the existing architecture and deciding the practical implementation yourself.

The target feeling is:

**You are sitting inside an SKM train travelling through the Tricity, looking through the window at Gdańsk passing by.**

It should feel lived-in, dense, atmospheric, slightly melancholic, and authentic.

### Authenticity

The environment should strongly communicate **Gdańsk / Tricity suburban railway**, rather than looking like a generic European train or generic pixel-art railway.

Study the visual language of the real environment and reproduce its important characteristics through the existing game's artistic style.

The train itself should feel like an actual Gdańsk SKM carriage: believable proportions, interior structure, windows, seats, doors, handles, lighting, materials, signage, small imperfections, and the general visual character of a commuter train.

The landscape should likewise feel recognizably local.

Do not make it an empty railway corridor. Think about the density and visual complexity of the real Tricity railway environment.

Avoid large areas that feel empty, unfinished, or procedurally generic.

Populate the environment with an appropriate mixture of:

The exact implementation and asset strategy are up to you.

Every layer should contribute to the perception of a real place.

### The view from the train

One of the most important goals is making the train window a genuinely enjoyable place to look through.

The player should be able to simply sit there and watch Gdańsk move past.

Create a strong sense of:

**foreground → railway infrastructure → nearby buildings → distant city → sky**

with appropriate depth and movement between layers.

The landscape should not simply scroll as one flat background.

Create convincing spatial separation and parallax where appropriate. Nearby objects should communicate speed and proximity differently from distant objects.

The passing scenery should feel like a continuous journey rather than a repeating game background.

### Visual depth and volume

The current presentation should be pushed substantially beyond flat graphics.

Use the existing rendering capabilities to create:

* convincing depth;
* layered composition;
* volumetric-looking forms;
* ambient shading;
* natural shadows;
* occlusion;
* atmospheric perspective;
* reflections;
* silhouettes;
* subtle lighting variation;
* foreground/background separation;
* and visual transitions between environmental layers.

Graphics are one of the primary building blocks of this game, so visual quality should be treated as a core gameplay and storytelling system.

### Train interior

The interior should feel like a real place where someone could spend time.

Do not stop at the major objects.

Look for opportunities to communicate the smaller details that make a commuter train believable: surfaces, seats, windows, frames, lighting, handles, doors, signage, reflections, wear, shadows, and subtle irregularities.

The player should feel surrounded by the environment rather than sitting inside an empty rectangular container.

### Melancholy and atmosphere

This is particularly important.

The train sequence should carry a **melancholic visual-novel atmosphere**.

Think about the emotional feeling of travelling alone by train while looking outside at the city.

The scene should support feelings such as:

* solitude;
* nostalgia;
* quietness;
* evening travel;
* homesickness;
* passing time;
* watching unfamiliar people and places through a window;
* and the strange calm of being temporarily disconnected from the world.

Do not achieve this primarily through explicit UI or text.

The **environment itself should create the emotion**.

Lighting, weather, sky, reflections, scenery, color relationships, movement, density, and pacing should collectively create the mood.

### Natural movement

The train journey should feel continuous and physical.

Consider how the scenery behaves as the train moves, how close objects pass by, how distant objects remain relatively stable, and how the train's own interior responds to movement.

The existing bidirectional train movement must remain supported.

The experience should remain coherent whether the train travels left or right, including:

* scenery;
* parallax;
* environmental layers;
* lighting;
* shadows;
* animation;
* foreground elements;
* and the relationship between the train and landscape.

Do not build the visual system around a single travel direction.

### Avoid artificial repetition

The landscape should not immediately reveal obvious repeating patterns.

Where repetition is unavoidable, use composition, variation, layering, timing, and environmental differences to make the journey feel natural.

The player should feel that the train is moving through a continuous part of Gdańsk rather than watching the same set of assets endlessly cycle.

### Visual-novel presentation

Remember that this game is fundamentally driven by atmosphere and visual storytelling.

The train journey should therefore be something the player can **look at and emotionally experience**, not merely a transportation mechanic.

There should be moments where the player can simply observe the scenery and enjoy the composition.

The scene should have intentional framing and visual hierarchy, almost like a sequence from a cinematic visual novel.

Do not prescribe a specific implementation.

First inspect `trainScene`, `trainLandscape`, and the systems they depend on.

Understand:

* the current rendering architecture;
* movement model;
* camera model;
* layering;
* existing visual utilities;
* asset conventions;
* animation systems;

* and how the train currently interacts with the landscape.

Then decide the best architecture and implementation yourself.

Reuse existing systems where they are strong. Refactor or replace weak approaches where necessary.

Do not add complexity merely for complexity's sake.

The goal is not to make the code larger.

The goal is to make the **experience dramatically richer**.

The final result should feel like a polished, atmospheric, authentic Gdańsk SKM journey inside a melancholic visual novel — a place where the player can genuinely enjoy sitting by the window and watching the world pass by.

Prioritize:

**authenticity → atmosphere → visual density → depth → environmental storytelling → natural movement → polish.**

Make the result feel **lived-in, emotionally evocative, and unmistakably Gdańsk**.
