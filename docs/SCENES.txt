Create a new production-quality scene:

`trainStation.tsx`

representing **Gdańsk Przymorze-Uniwersytet SKM station**, followed by a separate interactive:

`trainScene`

The experience should be grounded in a believable Gdańsk environment and should feel like a natural extension of the existing game world.

Use the existing `streetScene` as the **technical and visual reference** for scene architecture, composition, pixel-art treatment, lighting, scale, character rendering, environmental detail, animation, interaction patterns, and overall quality.

Do not simply copy `streetScene`. Use it to understand the project's established visual language and scene conventions.

---

# 1. Station Scene — `trainStation.tsx`

Build a complete SKM station environment representing the **Przymorze-Uniwersytet** area.

The player should immediately understand that they are standing on a real urban railway platform in Gdańsk.

The scene should contain:

* railway platform;
* tracks;
* platform edge;
* SKM infrastructure;
* station signage;
* shelters;
* benches;
* lamps;
* poles;
* railway equipment;
* electrical infrastructure;
* advertisements/posters;
* bins;
* small architectural details;
* pavement markings;
* platform safety markings;
* environmental clutter;
* distant buildings;
* realistic urban background.

Do not make the platform unnaturally clean or empty.

It should feel like a real station that people actually use.

---

# 2. Gdańsk Visual Identity

The location should be clearly recognizable as **Gdańsk**, rather than a generic railway station.

The surrounding environment should communicate the Przymorze/Oliva urban landscape.

A major visual landmark should be the **Alchemia office complex / Alchemia towers** visible in the background.

The towers should have:

* appropriate proportions;
* recognizable silhouette;
* believable distance;
* atmospheric perspective;
* appropriate lighting;
* correct visual scale relative to the station.

Do not place them unrealistically close to the platform.

They should feel like distant urban architecture visible beyond the station.

Add other background buildings and urban elements so the towers do not look like isolated sprites.

---

# 3. Station Composition

Build the scene in distinct depth layers:

### Foreground

The player, platform details, benches, signs, lamps, objects and other interactive elements.

### Midground

Tracks, railway infrastructure, waiting passengers and approaching trains.

### Background

Urban landscape, buildings, vegetation, roads and the Alchemia towers.

### Far background

Gdańsk skyline and atmospheric elements.

Use depth, occlusion, lighting and perspective to prevent the scene from feeling flat.

---

# 4. Platform Population

The station should not feel deserted.

Add several NPCs waiting on the platform.

They should have varied behavior.

Examples:

* standing and checking their phone;
* sitting on a bench;
* looking toward the tracks;
* talking to another person;
* walking along the platform;
* checking the timetable;
* waiting near the doors when the train arrives;
* casually looking around.

NPCs should not all perform the same animation simultaneously.

Use the existing character/NPC systems wherever possible.

Their behavior should be subtle and believable.

---

# 5. Ambient Station Animation

The station should be alive even when no train is present.

Consider subtle animation such as:

* people moving;
* flags/posters moving slightly;
* subtle lighting;
* distant traffic;
* environmental particles;
* birds;
* small background movement;
* electronic signage;
* occasional platform activity.

Keep ambient animation restrained.

The goal is realism and atmosphere, not visual noise.

---

# 6. Initial Train Activity

Before the player can interact with a train, there should be normal railway activity.

At appropriate intervals, an SKM train should appear in the environment and travel through the scene.

This first train does **not necessarily need to be interactive**.

It should function as environmental storytelling and establish that this is an active railway.

The train should:

1. appear from the appropriate direction;
2. approach the station/track;
3. move naturally through the scene;
4. produce appropriate visual/audio feedback;
5. continue toward its destination;
6. disappear into the distance.

The train should not simply teleport into existence.

Use smooth acceleration and deceleration.

---

# 7. Train Going Toward Sopot

At least one environmental train should visually communicate travel toward **Sopot**.

The player does not necessarily need to board this first train.

The purpose is to make the railway environment feel operational and establish the direction of travel.

Make the train movement convincing:

* correct relative scale;
* believable speed;
* motion consistency;
* natural entrance/exit;
* appropriate sound/effects if supported;
* subtle environmental reaction.

---

# 8. Interactive Train Event

After the player has spent some time on the platform, an **interactive modern Gdańsk SKM train** should arrive.

This should be a deliberate gameplay event.

Do not make the interactive train immediately available the moment the scene loads.

The player should have time to:

* explore;
* observe the environment;
* interact with the station;
* watch normal train traffic;
* experience the atmosphere.

Then the interactive train arrives.

---

# 9. Modern SKM Train

The interactive train should visually resemble a modern **Gdańsk SKM train**.

Pay attention to:

* train proportions;
* carriage geometry;
* windows;
* doors;
* headlights;
* exterior details;
* interior visibility;
* railway markings;
* appropriate colors/materials;
* pantograph/roof equipment where visible;
* realistic carriage connections.

Use the project's pixel-art style while retaining enough detail for the train to be immediately recognizable.

Do not create a generic metro train.

---

# 10. Train Arrival Sequence

The arrival should be fully animated.

Sequence:

1. distant train becomes visible;
2. train approaches;
3. speed decreases;
4. train enters the station;
5. train stops;
6. doors become interactive;
7. passengers may enter/exit;
8. player can approach a valid door;
9. interaction becomes available.

The player should be able to understand when boarding is possible through visual feedback rather than an unexplained UI state.

---

# 11. Train Interaction Zones

Do **not** make the entire train clickable.

Define specific interaction zones.

The primary interaction zones should be:

* carriage doors.

Potentially support other meaningful interaction points if appropriate, but keep the interaction model spatially believable.

The player should have to physically approach a door.

When close enough:

* show the appropriate interaction indicator;
* highlight the valid interaction zone;
* allow boarding.

Do not allow boarding from arbitrary locations along the train.

---

# 12. Boarding Transition

Boarding should have a dedicated transition.

Avoid an abrupt scene replacement.

The player should:

1. approach the train door;
2. interact;
3. play an appropriate boarding animation;
4. transition into the carriage;
5. enter `trainScene`.

Use lighting/audio/environment transitions where appropriate.

The transition should feel like the player physically entered the train.

---

# 13. `trainScene`

Create a separate scene:

`trainScene`

This should represent the **interior of a moving SKM carriage**.

This scene is extremely important.

It should feel different from normal static locations while remaining part of the same game world.

---

# 14. Train Interior

Create a believable modern train carriage interior containing:

* seats;
* rows of seats;
* windows;
* doors between carriages;
* handrails;
* poles;
* overhead elements;
* luggage/utility areas where appropriate;
* information displays;
* route information;
* interior lighting;
* carriage walls;
* floor;
* small realistic details.

The player should immediately understand:

> I am sitting inside a moving Gdańsk SKM train.

---

# 15. Interactive Train Interior

The carriage should be genuinely interactive.

Allow the player to:

* walk around;
* inspect/interact with appropriate objects;
* sit down;
* stand up;
* move between valid areas;
* interact with the route/map;
* look outside;
* choose where to travel.

Do not make every object interactive.

Use meaningful interaction zones.

---

# 16. Sitting

Implement a proper sitting interaction.

The player should be able to approach a seat and interact with it.

The sequence should be:

1. approach seat;
2. interaction indicator appears;
3. player selects the seat;
4. character transitions naturally into sitting;
5. camera/character state adjusts appropriately;
6. player can remain seated while the train moves;
7. player can stand up again.

Avoid instantaneous teleportation into a sitting pose.

Use an appropriate transition animation if the character system supports it.

---

# 17. Route Selection

The train should provide a special travel-selection interaction.

This should be represented by a **route map displayed inside the carriage**.

The map should look like a real railway route diagram integrated into the carriage.

It should allow the player to choose a destination.

At minimum, support the relevant Gdańsk/SKM travel directions and destinations supported by the game's world.

The route map should not look like a generic game menu.

It should physically exist as part of the carriage.

---

# 18. Map Interaction

When the player interacts with the route map:

* provide a clear interaction state;
* visually emphasize stations/destinations;
* allow the player to select a destination;
* show the selected route;
* confirm the choice;
* begin the corresponding travel sequence.

The interaction should preserve immersion.

Prefer an in-world presentation over opening an unrelated fullscreen UI.

---

# 19. Moving Train Experience

The train should actually feel like it is moving.

This is one of the most important aspects of the scene.

While inside the train:

* exterior scenery should move through the windows;
* lighting should change;
* distant buildings should pass;
* tracks/environment should create a sense of movement;
* occasional tunnels/structures can alter lighting;
* scenery should have different apparent speeds depending on distance;
* the train should have subtle movement/rocking.

Do not simply animate a generic scrolling background.

Use layered parallax to create convincing travel.

---

# 20. Gdańsk Landscape

The outside environment should represent the broader Gdańsk area.

A major background motif should be the **Gdańsk shipyard / Stocznia landscape**, including recognizable cranes and industrial infrastructure where appropriate.

Use:

* shipyard cranes;
* industrial buildings;
* railway infrastructure;
* distant urban architecture;
* rooftops;
* utility structures;
* vegetation;
* roads;
* skyline elements.

The player should occasionally see recognizable Gdańsk scenery through the train windows.

---

# 21. Train Movement & Landscape System

Create a system that can support multiple scenery segments.

For example:

* station departure;
* urban buildings;
* railway infrastructure;
* industrial/shipyard area;
* distant skyline;
* vegetation;
* bridges;
* tunnels;
* next station;
* destination arrival.

Use a combination of:

* parallax layers;
* animated foreground;
* midground movement;
* distant background movement;
* lighting changes.

The system should be extensible so additional destinations can be added later.

---

# 22. Atmosphere

The train journey should provide a different emotional experience from walking around the city.

It should feel:

* calm;
* observational;
* slightly melancholic;
* urban;
* atmospheric;
* grounded;
* distinctly Gdańsk.

The player should be able to simply sit and watch the world pass outside.

Do not overload the player with UI.

Let the environment carry the experience.

* animated environment;
* lighting;
* parallax;
* moving scenery;

* inactive NPCs;
* train rendering;

* memory;
* animation loops;

* Does the station feel like a real place?
* Does the Alchemia background feel correctly integrated?
* Do the NPCs feel natural?
* Does the train arrival feel believable?
* Are interaction zones intuitive?
* Does boarding feel physical?
* Does the train interior feel detailed?
* Does sitting feel natural?
* Does the route map feel like part of the train?
* Does the train actually feel like it is moving?
* Does the landscape look like Gdańsk?
* Does the shipyard/crane background create the intended atmosphere?

Then perform another improvement pass based on the findings.

# 28. Final Experience

The complete experience should be:

**Explore Gdańsk → arrive at Przymorze-Uniwersytet → wait on platform → observe station life → watch trains pass → interactive SKM arrives → approach a carriage door → board → enter trainScene → explore carriage → sit or walk → interact with route map → choose destination → train begins moving → watch Gdańsk landscapes and Stocznia cranes pass outside → arrive at destination.**

The result should feel like a **fully designed game experience**, not simply two connected scenes.

Prioritize realism, atmosphere, environmental storytelling, interaction quality, animation consistency, and technical quality.

Use `streetScene` as the quality and architectural reference, but push the new scenes further in terms of environmental detail, interactivity, animation, and atmosphere.
